# Chapter stories driven by hidden-object discoveries

Owner-directed design, 14 November 2026. The 36-room reward map below is now implemented in the hidden-object
campaign branch, including named map areas and chapter search gates. Story spoilers
below. Difficulty, pacing and final creative acceptance still require playtesting. Preserve the existing overall mystery and recurring cast.

## The player's journey

An overall mystery spans the game. Each chapter is a satisfying smaller story,
with its own immediate question, stakes, characters, discoveries, turning point
and resolution. Its ending both answers that local question and contributes a
necessary part of the overall explanation. The next chapter follows from what
the player has discovered.

The world map leads to chapter districts. Within a chapter, several areas each
contain a couple of distinct hidden-object rooms or close-up searches. The
working layout below uses **three areas with two search rooms apiece**, grouping
the existing six locations per chapter. This is a production starting point,
not a number dictated by the owner or a reason to add repetitive filler.

The main loop is:

**Explore an area → search a room → recover evidence or a useful item → use it
to resolve a room problem or connect clues → complete the chapter's mystery →
advance the overall story.**

Finding a list's decorative objects is the search activity. Its completion
rewards a concrete, relevant discovery. Every required search has a defined
purpose and reward; every reward has an identified later use. A key may open a
room; a damaged record may need reconstruction; a pattern may decode a signal;
a physical trace may contradict a witness. A search must not simply add a
completion percentage and a generic congratulatory sentence.

The player keeps collected evidence in a concise casebook. Room-level puzzles
use a small number of relevant discoveries, then contribute a clear conclusion
to the chapter's final deduction. Players should understand what they are
trying to solve and how their discoveries help. Present material changes in the
world—an opened passage, working signal or rescued record—alongside narrative
consequences.

## Chapter 1 — The Borrowed Bell

**Local story:** Find what happened to the missing archivist and follow her
trail through a rainbound harbour. Conflicting accounts turn an apparent
abduction into a more complicated investigation.

| Area | Search room | Completion reward | What it helps solve |
|---|---|---|---|
| Harbour approach | Ferry Landing (`ch1_l1`) | Dispatch receipt | Identify the parcel and connect Mara to the boarding-house records. |
| Harbour approach | Tide Office (`ch1_l2`) | Original tide-table strip and shutter mark | Establish the first contradiction in the official flood account. |
| Archive | Archive Vestibule (`ch1_l3`) | Displaced catalogue ticket and conservation supplies | Locate the removed folio and gain access to its damaged fragments. |
| Archive | Restoration Room (`ch1_l4`) | Recovered folio fragments | Reconstruct Mara's sequence of actions and her interest in the works. |
| Bell quarter | Boarding House (`ch1_l5`) | Timed visitor entry and borrowed-key record | Establish her deliberate itinerary and access to the bell tower. |
| Bell quarter | Bell Tower (`ch1_l6`) | Bell-pull fitting and signal strip | Restore the small signal mechanism and interpret the route she left. |

**Resolution:** Reconstruct a supported itinerary. The chapter closes its
missing-person trail question while exposing the flood/tide discrepancy that
makes the pumping works the next destination. Use a physical restoration and
signal payoff, not a final screen that merely repeats six pieces of text.

Suggested dependency structure: the two harbour searches open two branches;
receipt → boarding house → bell tower, and tide discrepancy → archive ticket →
restoration. The branches converge in the chapter deduction. Hinting explains
which lead remains useful rather than revealing every future location at once.

**Local story:** Investigate machinery and reluctant workers to establish how

The remaining timing question carries the player to the observatory.

**Local story:** Get the witnesses and evidence safely into the public record,

|---|---|---|---|
| Witness passage | Dawn Quay (`ch6_l1`) | Independent dispatch packets | Arrange separate routes for people and records. |

| Protected route | Relay House (`ch6_l3`) | Relay code and verification seal | Authenticate the independent delivery. |
| Protected route | Gate Crown (`ch6_l4`) | Safe-isolation fittings and final gate record | Secure passage and confirm the physical diversion mechanism. |

it must not withhold a crucial accusation until after the hearing. The epilogue
shows the consequences for the city and cast rather than opening an unrelated
mystery that prevents closure.

## Implementation and acceptance

* Add area grouping to the map; rooms/close-ups live within an area. Use a couple
  of distinct searches per area as the baseline. Additional revisits require a
  changed story context, artwork or objective; never reset the same list merely
  to stretch the game.
* Author every search reward, its prerequisites and its downstream use before
  creating final art. The table's proposed rewards must be reconciled with the
  existing runtime item IDs, evidence sources and dialogue reveal order.
* Persist partial lists, rewarded searches, room solutions and chapter
  conclusions. Award each clue/item once, preserve revisits, and migrate old
  saves deliberately. Completing a search and leaving immediately must not
  lose its reward.
* Stage excitement through anticipation, discovery, character reversals,
  environmental changes and chapter finales. Keep scenes varied in setting,
  concealment and clue interpretation. Preserve the grounded mystery and
  non-graphic tone; no arbitrary timers or newly invented combat are implied.
* Update the dependency map as progression is implemented. The old rule that
  every finale requires nine text-heavy challenges is the alpha's baseline,
  not a design requirement for this search-driven version.
* Verify each required search has a useful reward and each mandatory deduction
  has discoverable evidence. Check both branch orders, wrong interpretations,
  mid-search saves and reward idempotency. Then test real first-time pacing and
  chapter satisfaction; authored counts do not prove six to ten hours.

The current standalone preview demonstrates only searching and a receipt
completion panel. It does not yet implement this chapter/area/reward structure.
